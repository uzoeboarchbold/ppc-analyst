## Part B — Plain-English summary

**The big picture.** These were two very quiet days. Across all 11 Sponsored
Products campaigns the account was shown **84 times**, earned **1 click**, and
spent **$2.84**. There were **no orders and no sales**, so ACOS can't be
calculated and ROAS is **0.00** for the period. In short: almost nothing
happened, and the tiny amount that was spent did not convert.

**Why so quiet.** Only 7 of the 11 campaigns got any impressions at all, and
just **one** campaign spent any money. Impression volume is extremely low
(84 impressions over two whole days), which usually points to bids sitting
below the market clearing price and/or a very small daily budget (this US
account's daily budget is set to **$40**). The ads are barely entering
auctions, so there is almost no traffic to convert.

**Best campaign.** Hard to crown a winner with no sales, but on *visibility*
the strongest was **"SP Isolated … Pet Odor Eliminator Phrase"** — it captured
a **22% top-of-search impression share** and was the only campaign to earn a
click. That tells us the keyword *pet odor eliminator* is relevant and can win
placement; it just needs to convert.

**Worst / wasted spend.** The only spend of the period — **$2.84 on 1 click
with no sale** — came from that same campaign, via the search term
**"pet odor eliminator spray."** It's a single click, so it's far too early to
call the term a loser, but it is the one place money left the account with
nothing to show. Watch it; don't negative it yet.

**Search terms worth adding as exact-match.** None qualify this period. Adding
an exact-match keyword is worth doing once a term has produced sales (or at
least several clicks). We have one click and zero sales, so there is no
converting term to promote yet. Revisit once volume builds.

**Negatives to add.** None recommended yet. One click with no order is not
enough evidence to exclude a term — negating "pet odor eliminator spray" now
would throw away a relevant, on-target search before we know how it performs.

### What to do next
1. **Increase visibility on the winner.** The *pet odor eliminator* phrase
   campaign already wins 22% of top-of-search — nudge its bids up modestly so
   it enters more auctions and gathers enough clicks to judge conversion.
2. **Check bids on the zero-impression campaigns.** Four campaigns got 0
   impressions and several got only 1–2. Their bids are almost certainly too
   low to compete — review and raise where the targets are still relevant.
3. **Confirm the budget isn't the bottleneck.** $40/day is fine for this
   traffic level, but verify no campaign is capped early in the day.
4. **Let it run and gather data.** With only 84 impressions there simply isn't
   enough signal yet to prune terms or add exacts. The priority for the next
   window is *more qualified traffic*, then optimise on conversions.
